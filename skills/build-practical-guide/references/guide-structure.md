# Self-Study Unit Structure

Use this reference for a new self-study unit or a substantial restructuring of
an existing unit. The unit is the default deliverable for one connected
learning outcome. It may be long when the approved scope needs depth. Do not
remove necessary reasoning to make it shorter.

## Teaching Route

Build one continuous route through the subject. “Narrative” means that each
idea answers a question raised by the preceding idea; it does not require a
fictional story or anecdote.

Use the sections that serve the agreed learning outcome, in an order such as:

1. **Learning contract** — Intended reader, outcome, relevant prerequisites,
   and how the unit fits the larger knowledge map.
2. **Opening question or problem** — Establish what needs explaining or doing
   and why the reader needs the material.
3. **Prerequisite bridge** — Explain or link the knowledge the reader needs;
   do not hide a necessary dependency behind assumed expertise.
4. **Conceptual foundations** — Introduce terms, models, assumptions, and the
   reason each one is needed.
5. **Reasoning and evidence** — Develop the theory, derivation, method, or
   argument step by step. Connect claims to evidence and make intermediate
   logic visible.
6. **Worked examples or cases** — Walk through representative cases, showing
   the choices and reasoning rather than presenting only the result.
7. **Variations, counterexamples, and limitations** — Explain where the idea
   changes, fails, remains contested, or should not be applied.
8. **Synthesis and continuation** — Reconnect the unit's ideas and link to the
   next prerequisite, dependent topic, or optional branch.
9. **Sources and lookup aids** — Provide traceable references and useful
   terminology, indexes, and cross-links.

For a theoretical subject, the route may move from a motivating question to
definitions, assumptions, derivations, examples, counterexamples, and
implications. For a practical subject, it may move from a real task through its
mental model, method, worked case, verification, failure modes, and limits.
Preserve all parts required by the approved scope; this outline is not a reason
to omit material.

## Optional Depth and Navigation

- Keep the full approved content. Mark foundational material, the recommended
  main route, optional depth, disputed areas, and explicit exclusions.
- Use cross-links, glossary entries, and an index for lookup. They supplement
  the continuous teaching route; they do not replace it.
- Use a single Markdown file when the full route remains navigable there. Split
  into modules when distinct outcomes or prerequisite clusters justify it.
- For file output, follow the approved directory tree and link each unit to its
  relevant prerequisites, related topics, sources, and next step.
- Do not add mandatory problem sets. Prefer worked examples, complete
  derivations, annotated cases, and reasoning traces. A short reflection prompt
  is optional only when it clearly supports understanding and adds little
  burden.

## Teaching a Procedure

For each consequential operational step, explain:

- What to do and why it is necessary.
- How important terms, parameters, and side effects work.
- What result to expect and how to verify it safely.
- What to check when the result differs, without inventing a cause.
- Recovery or rollback guidance when persistent changes are involved.

Do not mechanically repeat a template when connected prose is clearer. Keep
the reasoning, expected result, and verification observable.

## Accuracy and Scope

- Introduce technical terms before relying on them, at the depth this reader
  needs.
- Preserve assumptions, intermediate reasoning, evidence, limitations,
  counterexamples, and meaningful disagreement inside the approved boundary.
- Distinguish verified claims, sourced interpretations, inference, and unknown
  information.
- Prefer authoritative primary sources for original claims and technical
  behavior. Use established textbooks and reviews for appropriate synthesis.
- Do not claim that citations alone prove correctness; check whether the source
  supports the claim and whether the reasoning follows.

## Quality Check

Confirm that a reader who did not see the original conversation can:

- State what question or capability the unit addresses and what it assumes.
- Follow why each major idea is introduced and how it connects to the next.
- Understand the evidence and reasoning behind important conclusions.
- Follow a worked example or case without hidden intermediate steps.
- Identify limitations, open questions, and conditions where the material does
  not apply.
- Find prerequisites, related topics, and sources through working links.
- Tell which content is core, optional, disputed, or outside the approved scope.
